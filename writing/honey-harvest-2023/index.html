Not a fun harvest

Tim Beauchamp

Sep 10, 2023

This is only my 4th year of beekeeping, so I have yet to get my groove set, but yesterday was not a fun harvest for me, and certainly not for my bees. To be fair, harvest day probably is never the highlight of the season for them, but it has always been enjoyable and gone fairly well for me.

A big challenge for me is that my hives sit down a steep slope at the back of my property and carrying a super with full honeyed frames up the hill to the garage is quite a workout. So, this year, I decided to use a spare nuc to bring up 5 frames at a time. Smart, right? After the first trip with the first 5 frames, I was pretty sure I was a genius. That turned out to be premature.

Filling the nuc with 5 frames after coaxing off the confused bees, covering the hive back up, lugging 5 frames of honey up the hill was a piece of cake. I then transferred the frames into a spare box in the garage and headed back down the hill to the hive.

The girls were a little bit annoyed with me when I got back down there. A couple puffs from the smoker seemed to distract them enough to allow me to remove the final 5 frames from the top super, sweep off the confused workers, and bring my bounty up to the garage.

Great, half done, one more super of 10 frames to go. That is when things went south.

As I got back down to the hive, they were waiting for me and greeted me before I even got the top cover off. By that point, the smoker was not helping much. I managed to get the next 5 frames out, and into the nuc, but the sound of tiny wings around my bonnet was deafening. The bees were on me like ugly on an ape. As I got to a center frame, I came to the additional conclusion that my choice to not use a queen excluder this year as an experiment was a failure. I had to leave that one in because her majesty had some of her future worker larva tucked in it. Oh well, I’d end up leaving two frames that were serving as bee nurseries in the last super.

That is when a few bees found the spot at the bottom of my bee jacket that had come untucked from my bee pants. At first, they were just walking around, exploring the untanned patch of bare skin. But they soon got bored and committed their suicide mission leaving three little stingers in the base of my back. That is when I got pissed off. I brought that third load up to the garage with a fair bit of grumbling to myself and colorful words for them.

I decided I needed to change into my more industrial bee suit for the final trip down. Good choice because they again met me at the hive. They obviously had had enough of my poking and prodding and were very vocal about it. Also, by this point, the smoker had already given its final puff and was of no use. I loaded up 3 of the final frames because the other two had brood in them, filled the super up with 8 new, empty drawn comb frames. Hopefully that gesture on my part will appease the colony.

I brought that last full comb up to the garage, but this time my angry victims did not feel they were done with me. I am pretty sure I had a fair bit of angry bee pheromone on me, because what felt like half the hive followed me up to the driveway. They were so persistent that I didn’t feel comfortable enough to take the suit off. So, I walked around a bit fully donned and vailed until the angry ladies gave up on me and they went back down to their hive to survey my damage of my pillaging.

I finally doffed the protective suit and spent the next 20 minutes cooling down, both physically and emotionally, on the deck inside a screened in patio table with a cold adult beverage. There was obviously still enough of their tagging pheromone on me because no matter which side of the table I sat, there were always a dozen of my angry little pets right behind me on the other side of the netting.

Eventually, I got to successfully extracting about 35 lbs. of beautiful golden local honey, but what a day.
